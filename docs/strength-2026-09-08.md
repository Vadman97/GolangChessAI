# September 8, 2026 playing-strength investigation

Source: `/tmp/chess.lichess.log`, ending with game `5JLfZAn3` at
2026-09-08 16:21:58 America/Los_Angeles. The six inspected losses were
`qlDlv2bD`, `RrxHqA6J`, `Lmd5Up7B`, `KO5FXfxz`, `Op67WYFO`, and `5JLfZAn3`.
Positions were reconstructed from the Lichess UCI move streams, preserving
castling and en-passant rights. Analysis used the local Stockfish binary.

## Opening search budget

In `5JLfZAn3`, after `1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nf6 3.Nxe5 d6 4.Nf3`, the bot played
`4...Nbd7` with 901.68 seconds remaining. The server restricted its search to
1.2 seconds because it was still in the first four turns. Stockfish depth 16
assessed the logged move as a 189 cp loss; `4...Nxe4` was needed.

Exact position:

```text
rnbqkb1r/ppp2ppp/3p1n2/8/4P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/RNBQKB1R b KQkq - 1 4
```

Current-source diagnosis before changes:

| Search | Selected move | Stockfish loss |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Fixed depth 4, 1 or 8 workers | Nxe4 | 4 cp |
| Fixed depth 5, 1 or 8 workers | Nc6 | 121–126 cp |
| Fixed depth 6, 1 or 8 workers | Nxe4 | 4 cp |
| 1.2 seconds, 8 workers, 3 runs | Nc6 in all runs | 121 cp mean |
| 15 seconds, 8 workers, 3 runs | Nxe4 in all runs | 3 cp mean |

Individually disabling LMR, null move, futility pruning, or razoring at depth 5
did not recover the recapture. This is a horizon-sensitive move, and the fixed
opening time cap prevented the engine from using its healthy clock.

Removed that special cap. Searches now use the normal early-game allocation
and configured/dynamic maximum; scripted opening replies still return
immediately. Clock reserves, low-time allocation, and critical-position
extensions continue to apply.

The new `testdata/lichess_opening_search_fens.txt` records 11 opening-search
positions from the six losses. The scripted Petroff `3...d6` position is
excluded because it never calls search. Each position was searched twice at
the former 1.2-second limit and twice at the budget for its game's initial
clock (3, 5, 10, or 15 seconds). Worker counts follow the engine's existing
default policy: four below 10 seconds, eight at or above 10 seconds.
These approximate early-game budgets do not replay clock consumption over an
entire game. Both policies used the same search binary and Stockfish depth 16.

The clock-only change reduced the mean position loss from **24.7 cp to 9.0 cp**.
Positions with mean loss at least 100 cp fell from **one to zero**.
Two positions became modestly worse (9 to 38 cp and 16 to 30 cp), so this is an
aggregate gain, not a claim that more time always selects a better move.

## Rejected search experiment: incomplete root verification

The timed comparison exposed a second issue. All root moves could finish at
depth 5, after which optional close-root verification used the rest of the
clock. Iterative deepening checked only the global abort flag and discarded
the completed depth-5 result, falling back to depth 4.

An experiment returned root completion separately from the abort flag,
retaining the unverified completed move/score when the tie-break timed out.
Deterministic tests passed and `Lmd5Up7B` before White's fourth move improved:
three 5-second searches retained depth 5 and selected `dxc5`, averaging 18 cp
loss, versus the clock-only binary's depth-4 `Nc3` at 30 cp over two runs.

However, the full 11-position comparison rejected the experiment. Mean loss
rose from 9.0 to 28.1 cp. The Petroff position accepted an unverified depth-7
`Nc6` at 123 cp loss instead of the verified depth-6 `Nxe4`. Another position
rose from 0 to 94 cp loss. Merely retaining a completed root iteration is not
enough: the verifier materially changes the trustworthiness of close root
choices. The experiment and its tests were reverted. Search behavior remains
unchanged; only the server's opening clock allocation is modified.

## Remaining evidence

These are local move-quality results, not an Elo estimate. New games with the
rebuilt engine are still needed to measure practical results and clock use.
No Lichess bot process was running during the investigation; the pre-existing
`main` process was the web frontend.

Final validation: `go test ./pkg/chessai/server ./pkg/chessai/analysis -count=1`
and `git diff --check` passed. `go build -o main ./cmd/main.go` rebuilt the bot;
its SHA-256 matched the clock-only binary used for the successful comparison.
The experimental search changes are absent from the final diff. The broader
search benchmark was stopped after the opening comparison rejected them; it
is not counted as a passed validation.
